## Version and connection evidence

Record exact **running** daemon/core/build versions and auto-update configuration immediately before and after each sequence, plus installed versions. The saved baseline has Hyperion's installed formula 0.81.5 and running daemon 0.80.0; iPhone UI/core/build can differ. Do not incidentally activate a pending upgrade. Capture each router's P2P/relay/idle status before/after every loss and return from `netbird status -d` or matching client diagnostics; record whether the survivor was idle when the active peer failed.

Source expectation is version-scoped. Inspected [0.79.0 route selection](https://github.com/netbirdio/netbird/blob/v0.79.0/client/internal/routemanager/client/client.go) considers connected/idle state, metric, latency and P2P/relay, with 0.01 score hysteresis. In that version status/relay transitions trigger selection; latency updates alone do not. The same scoring/hysteresis appears in inspected [0.80.0 selection](https://github.com/netbirdio/netbird/blob/v0.80.0/client/internal/routemanager/client/client.go) and [0.81.5 selection](https://github.com/netbirdio/netbird/blob/v0.81.5/client/internal/routemanager/client/client.go), but its subscription implementation differs. This is a comparison of the route-selection function and watcher constants, not a complete client audit or a match to the phone's exact core/build. Verify matching public source where available when the actual versions are known; otherwise retain the uncertainty and measure that client independently.

The [0.79.0 WireGuard watcher](https://github.com/netbirdio/netbird/blob/v0.79.0/client/internal/peer/wg_watcher.go) has a 3-minute handshake period plus 30 seconds overtime; [0.80.0](https://github.com/netbirdio/netbird/blob/v0.80.0/client/internal/peer/wg_watcher.go) and [0.81.5](https://github.com/netbirdio/netbird/blob/v0.81.5/client/internal/peer/wg_watcher.go) retains those constants with changed watcher lifecycle. Abrupt failure may take about 3.5 minutes by that fallback, while ICE/relay can detect sooner. These source constants are not an observed outage bound. A successful 60-second graceful test must never be presented as an abrupt-loss guarantee.

A returning P2P router may displace a relayed survivor. Later relay/P2P state changes can also switch the NAT source and reset established TCP **without a failure**. Do not assume automatic failback to the oldest router, or interpret every TCP reset as failed HA. Record the actual path for every resource and client. No client toggle/metric change may force a passing row.

## Measurement and proposed acceptance bounds

The issue requires timings but does not yet specify a numerical H1 deadline. For graceful guest shutdown/service loss only, **propose 60 seconds** from confirmed active-peer loss to the first of three consecutive successful new DNS/HTTPS probe cycles through the survivor, and **120 seconds** for the restarted router to rejoin. The 120-second rejoin bound matches #625/R1; Matt/parent must accept the proposed 60-second H1 criterion before running. The 60-second recovery and 120-second rejoin criteria are proposed and unaccepted, not measured results or NetBird guarantees. They do not apply to abrupt host/guest loss.

Record: `t_command`, `t_peer_loss` (NetBird process/VM/peer disconnected), `t_old_path_last_success`, `t_new_path_first_packet`, `t_new_path_first_success`, and the first three-cycle stable result. Report both command-to-recovery and peer-loss-to-recovery, blackout duration, probe interval/timeouts, and any uncertainty; a 2-second probe cadence cannot resolve subsecond cutover. For restart, record `t_start`, `t_management_connected`, `t_client_connection_ready`, and any route-selection change. Do not stop observing immediately after a single success.

Pass requires all three resources to work through the surviving router within the accepted bound, no client repair, Main remaining direct and healthy, and unchanged I1 denials. Existing TCP is expected to stall/reset when the NAT source changes; its original session need not survive, but the chosen application's new connection must appear automatically and its recovery time must be recorded. If the application requires a user action, distinguish successful NetBird route recovery from failed application reconnection; do not mark H1 fully passed under the stated automatic-reconnection outcome.

## Test sequence — only one unavailable router at a time

1. **Baseline, 5 minutes:** with both routers up, prove each resource's selected router with client/packet evidence. Keep Main controls, away probes and the long-lived application running. Do not call `.103` primary merely because it existed first.
2. **First failure, A → B:** A is whichever router the away client currently uses. Confirm the other router B is healthy and its paths passed. On A's actual host, issue the approved clean guest shutdown: `sudo qm shutdown <A-VMID> --timeout 60`. Inspect result and stopped state. If shutdown times out, stop the test and diagnose; hard stop, host reboot and forced power loss are not approved fallbacks. A loss is the timed event; show B's `wt0` traffic and `.104` or `.103` egress source for every resource. Observe at least three stable success cycles and the automatic application reconnection. Hold the stable surviving path for two minutes.
3. **Return A / observe failback:** `sudo qm start <A-VMID>` on its host; require unchanged version/identity, active services and group participation within 120 seconds. Observe selected path and application behavior for five minutes. With an equal-metric group, a healthy returning router may stay unused. Record **no automatic switch observed** in that case; zero failback time is not a measurement. If the client switches back, record its timing, NAT-source change, TCP reset and automatic application reconnection.
4. **Reverse active failure, B → A:** once both routers are healthy, identify the active router again. The other numbered router must actually be active before its shutdown can count as reverse-failure coverage. Usually the client remains on B after step 3, allowing the symmetric shutdown/start of B with A serving. Apply the same timings, two-minute survivor dwell, I1 checks and five-minute return observation. Restore B before any further outage.
5. **If A immediately reclaimed the route:** B → A was observed as failback, but B's failure was not tested. Try the other already-approved away client if it naturally selects B, or use a later natural connection opportunity; repeat the active-path check. If no client uses B while A is ready, leave **reverse active-failure unverified** and regroup with parent before authorizing an additional controlled test mechanism. Do not stop an inactive B and claim failover; do not stop both, change metrics, or toggle a client to force a pass. This limitation is explicit because equal-metric routing is not a primary/standby switch.
6. **R1 service baseline:** separately exercise the issue's stop/start of the NetBird service on each router's installed version, one at a time, with the other healthy. Stop the chosen service for two minutes, then start it; record rejoin within 120 seconds, survivor selection, Main path, return behavior and lack of client repair. Run the service commands only via that guest's agent on its correct PVE host, and restore/validate it before touching the other. This is a planned R1 test, not part of the research or a router upgrade.
7. **Client coverage:** do the timed sequence with Hyperion away on the phone's hotspot and the phone on Main; repeat an iPhone-cellular/application sequence with Hyperion on Main so iOS route behavior is not inferred from macOS. Matt stays home for both. Preserve native T3 behavior and check it remains usable or reconnects automatically without a bind change; it is independent of the routing-peer data path. Persephone needs only the parent-owned away spot-check after parity, not a duplicated matrix.
8. **Finish:** both routers running, onboot/protection retained, identities/versions unchanged, all readbacks and endpoint results correct, test files/captures stopped, no temporary firewall/Cloud changes left behind. Parent records observed H1/R1 results in existing #1097/#625 and updates durable docs after its review. Do not close either issue on the strength of a planned test.

## Abort and recovery

Abort on survivor failure, missing local recovery, unexpected version/identity change, widened access, unresponsive guest shutdown or inability to restore the first router. Restore the most recently stopped router through its correct PVE host, verify Main and away paths, and do not proceed to the second failure. If a client needs an off/on toggle or daemon restart to recover, that recovery is allowed as an emergency **after** recording a failed row; it must not be presented as a pass. Hard stop or host power-cycle needs a separate owner decision.

A graceful guest shutdown demonstrates controlled router loss, not the detection time for an abrupt host crash, guest freeze, blocked upstream, or rack blackout. H1 does not establish backend-path health detection. P6 waits for the next separately approved planned host boot and must record NAS availability plus unattended guest start. Do not reboot a host, disrupt NFS or start a backup solely for this plan.

A connected router with a broken downstream path may remain selected. Local snippets address the intended startup dependency, not full HA. P6 and restore drills remain open.
