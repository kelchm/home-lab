# GLM-5.3-Flash recipes through SparkRun

These recipes serve `GLM-5.3-Flash-EXL3` across two DGX Sparks at `http://10.32.21.31:8888/v1`. SparkRun 0.3.10 owns model/image distribution, fabric detection, containers, runtime caches, readiness, warmup, logs, stop, and native benchmarking for both engines. TensorFold uses the adjacent external runtime adapter. These hosts are outside Flux. Check actual host state before launching; other experiments can use either GPU. Two [Qwen3.8-Flash-Next profiles](#qwen38-flash-next-on-mias-zig-engine-kit) on Mia's Zig-engine kit sit beside them; the NVFP4 one has been serving since its first boot on October 8.

The September 30/October 1 candidates are under evaluation in [#645](https://github.com/kelchm/home-lab/issues/645) and [PR #648](https://github.com/kelchm/home-lab/pull/648). The versioned Mia TensorFold profiles retain the measured v1.2 config, the v1.5 and v1.8 server candidates on the same stock TR3 weights, and the v1.5, v1.8 and v1.10 published TP2 defaults with Mia’s own weights. Since 16:07 EDT on October 8 the Sparks run `glm53-exl3-tp2-mia-tf-v1.10-mia-1m` for operator evaluation. It is [source-verified](#v110-mia-weights-candidate) and passed its native boot and readiness gate, the source fixtures inside both ranks and a short vision, agent, cut-call and Anthropic-protocol gate; a [finite 44-minute study](#october-8-finite-study) then completed all 124 latency requests with no failed check, restart or kernel fault line. Against v1.8 that study shows no obvious regression and no large change on short sampled requests, lower long-prompt timings at one to three repetitions that need repeating, and one unexplained 29 s stall in a four-stream replay. Its [live record](#october-8-boot-and-short-gates) and the study list what is and is not established. It is not an adopted recipe and has no formal qualification. `glm53-exl3-tp2-mia-tf-v1.8-mia-1m` ran from October 7 EDT until it was stopped for this trial; its [live record](#october-7-live-boot-of-the-v18-mia-weights-profile) and run-in stay below, and its retained recipe is the immediate rollback. The other three v1.5 and v1.8 profiles are source-verified and have not started on the Sparks. v1.2, which ran until the October 7 switch, passed native boot/shape warmup, the live API/client gates, all 13 long-session cases and actual C4 output growth on October 1. The intermediate v1.3 candidate was source-verified but never live-qualified; its only launch aborted before containers started. Refreshed Mia/vLLM v7 passed native boot, CUDA/protocol and client-accounting checks, but only 18/20 agent cases. Its 13-case comparison completed with valid telemetry; headroom was below target and the short arrival during long prefill took 10.35s. TensorFold W20 remains a diagnostic comparison, and the exact September 19 Mia recipe, image and prepared mod remain the older retained fallback; the retained v1.8 recipe is the immediate rollback from the v1.10 trial, and the original v1.2 recipe, retained with its mod, image and weights, is the proven fallback behind it. Provisional trials require fresh healthy-link/idle checks and a finite guard: a recovered management-link drop is recorded and its timings excluded, 30 s of a continuously unhealthy link or missing telemetry ends the load, and a confirmed fatal condition or sustained sub-2 GiB headroom stops the candidate; the separate defect in [#649](https://github.com/kelchm/home-lab/issues/649) remains open. These are evaluation candidates; raw receipts stay outside git.

This compares complete recipes: the draft weights, arithmetic, scheduler and cache implementation differ. Normal evaluation is C4 at 128k–262k, with C1/C2 controls. Four 262k prompts plus reserved output exceed W20's shared pool and must queue. Mia TensorFold allocates prompt extents, then grows them during decode; its measured startup pool is required before making a capacity claim. C8 is a secondary short/warm workload. None of these recipes promises four simultaneous 850k requests.

W20's output reservations matter before that boundary: four 250k prompts with 32,768 output tokens and the 64-token reserve require at least 1,131,328 pool tokens. An 8,192-token output budget puts that arithmetic total at 1,033,024. The 256-token primary timing cases do not qualify C4 at the client's full 32k output limit. The full suite includes a 250k case with that budget and a short expected answer. Mia's kit does not reserve that whole budget at admission, so a separate growth case requires actual long output. TensorFold v1.2 completed four 250k prompts with 4,096 actual output tokens each; full 32k replies remain unqualified. Inspect admission, pool growth, pauses and tail latency before choosing client defaults.

### Versioned Mia TensorFold profiles

The `mia-tensorfold-glm53-exl3-tp2/` directory groups the shared Mia TensorFold server kit, GLM-5.3-Flash model, EXL3 format and TP2 topology. Filenames repeat the model, format and topology for identification outside the folder, followed by Mia’s TensorFold kit release (`mia-tf-v1.x`), quantization creator and request context ceiling: `glm53-exl3-tp2-mia-tf-v1.2-bmm-850k`, `glm53-exl3-tp2-mia-tf-v1.5-bmm-850k`, `glm53-exl3-tp2-mia-tf-v1.5-mia-1m`, `glm53-exl3-tp2-mia-tf-v1.8-bmm-850k`, `glm53-exl3-tp2-mia-tf-v1.8-mia-1m` and `glm53-exl3-tp2-mia-tf-v1.10-mia-1m`. The version is the kit release whose image the recipe pins, not the engine version: v1.5, v1.8 and v1.10 all build on TensorFold 0.6.0 and differ in Mia’s patch set. BMM is Brandon M. Music ([Hugging Face: `brandonmusic`](https://huggingface.co/brandonmusic)), whose TR3 checkpoint is mirrored by Mia; `mia` selects Mia’s own calibration, the kit’s standard checkpoint. The `850k` recipes allow 850,000 tokens with an 18.5 GiB reserve; `1m` allows 1,048,576 tokens with the published 14.5 GiB reserve and window settings. Each file is a complete recipe. SparkRun derives its name from the filename stem, which matches `metadata.profile` and remains unique across the six recipes; select it by its path under the family directory.

`metadata.upstream_release`, `metadata.weights`, `metadata.weights_variant` and `metadata.serving_profile` record the kit release, quantization creator, checkpoint variant and settings preset separately; all six use EXL3 at 4bpw. `recipe_version: '2'` is SparkRun’s schema version and stays 2 in all six files. All profiles keep the served API name `GLM-5.3-Flash-EXL3`, so the same client requests work after switching the active recipe. Use one profile at a time on the same two hosts.

The server was not stopped and nothing about it was changed. This is 72 minutes of load evidence, not an eight-hour stability result. A separate continuation then ran the rest of the night to its deadline and is recorded [below](#continuation-from-0308-to-0935-edt); the two runs are not pooled.

Every request that finished before the abort passed:

Limits of this run:

- There is no matched baseline. The prompts, sizes and client differ from the October 1 sweeps, so none of these times is a speed-up or a slow-down against v1.2 or vLLM.
- It does not cover long-session growth, the 262k boundary, a near-1M request, replies longer than 256 tokens, a fifth request or 429, or long-context coding quality. The tool and agent results are small protocol gates.
- The server wrote nothing to its rank log or container log during the run, so the guard's log scan had no text to match. Faults were watched through `/health`, container state, memory and the kernel journal.
- The client is one-off and is not in the repository. It bounds each batch itself and does not fix the long-session client's cancellation defect ([#651](https://github.com/kelchm/home-lab/issues/651)).
- An earlier native SparkRun throughput diagnostic at 00:54 EDT lost worker telemetry and is excluded in full. Its llama-benchy prompt-rate figure is also invalid for this server: it times the first SSE event, and TensorFold sends a role chunk before prefill.

paste <(rails) <(ssh "$worker" "$(declare -f rails); rails") | while read -r hh hn ha hm wh wn wa wm; do
  mtu=$(( hm < ${wm:-0} ? hm : ${wm:-0} ))
  if [[ "$ha" != - && -n "$wa" && "$wa" != - ]] && ping -c2 -W2 -I "$hn" -M do -s $((mtu - 28)) "$wa" >/dev/null 2>&1 &&

    echo "PASS rail $hh/$hn $ha <-> $wh/$wn $wa at MTU $mtu"
  else
    echo "FAIL rail $hh/$hn $ha <-> ${wh:--}/${wn:--} ${wa:--}"

##### Rollout procedure

All seven steps were performed on October 8 and are recorded above, step 7 as the [finite study](#october-8-finite-study). The next step is the operator’s: real coding and tool work through the existing OpenCode client, with its limits unchanged. This is a provisional trial under the same conditions as v1.8: [#649](https://github.com/kelchm/home-lab/issues/649) is open, and the 6 h 27 min v1.8 run-in without a host or engine fault does not close it. The launch and the load each run under a private finite guard kept with the receipts on the head, not `benchmarks/long_sessions.py`, whose cancellation defect is [#651](https://github.com/kelchm/home-lab/issues/651). Nothing restarts, rolls back or selects a recipe automatically.

1. Let the finite v1.8 control study finish. Confirm the rollback inputs are intact: the v1.8 recipe and mod, the v1.8 image `cbb4b3c6…`, the Mia and drafter snapshots, and the v1.8 runtime cache on both hosts.
2. Record a fresh baseline on both hosts: management links up at 10GbE with their carrier counters, MemAvailable, GPU processes, and v1.8 `/health` strict, `ok: true` and idle. Do not continue on a link that dropped since the pre-stage without a new quiet period.
3. Stop v1.8 with its own recipe and confirm both GPUs are free and memory has returned.
4. Launch v1.10 once under the boot guard described below. `run --no-follow` returns after the readiness hook: four streams, the 1,048,576 window, at least 1.2M pool tokens and C4 shape warmup. The first boot compiles into an empty image-keyed kernel cache.
5. Compare each rank’s `NCCL_IB_HCA`, which the boot guard records, between the two hosts and with the rails probed in the pre-stage, and read the rail count from the rank logs. A mismatch in order or count is a reason to stop and fix it, not to measure.
6. Run the three source fixtures inside each rank’s container, where the v1.10 regression reads the recipe’s environment as the rank has it. Then the short gate used for v1.8: five images and the short arrival, the 20 agent cases and both cut-call cases, reading per-case rows.
7. Add what v1.10 changes: sampled against greedy decode rate at the checkpoint’s default sampling, which is the purpose of the nucleus setting; decode rate and cold prefill at long context against v1.8’s figures for the same payloads; one `/v1/messages` tool turn; and a chunked-body request. Record the startup pool and both hosts’ memory minima.

The boot guard makes one native launch in its own process group and allows it 900 s. Before launching it requires that nothing answers on the port, that neither GPU has a compute process, that no container of the v1.10 image exists, that both management links are up at 10GbE and that each host has at least 100 GiB available. It then samples both hosts every 5 s and records the first rank container seen on each host as that host’s original. It keeps the server only when SparkRun exits 0; it then checks strict idle health, both original ranks, memory and the kernel journal for the whole launch, and reports anything it could not read as an open item with the server left running. Every other ending stops the candidate, because a server that is not ready has no use: a nonzero exit, the deadline, a rank still absent after 120 s or later lost or replaced, a fatal line in a rank log or the kernel journal, HTTP 503 with `ok: false` or a wrong shape from `/health`, a host reboot, three consecutive samples below 2 GiB available, or 30 s of a continuously unhealthy management link or missing telemetry. Stopping means ending the launch group and then taking a fresh inventory of both hosts. `sparkrun stop` addresses a workload by the recipe’s runtime, model, image, port, served name and parallelism, not by container ID, so the guard runs it only when that inventory shows nothing but the original ranks on their original boot, or an original gone with no other v1.10 container in its place. When a rank was replaced or never recorded, a host has more than one v1.10 container, a host rebooted or its inventory could not be read, `sparkrun stop` is skipped and only the original rank IDs are stopped, each rechecked on its host against the recorded boot, container and image IDs. Any other v1.10 container is reported and left running, nothing on a rebooted host is stopped, and nothing is restarted. A rank left running this way keeps its GPU memory until it is dealt with by hand. The decision and each host’s reading are written to `stop-gate.json` before anything is stopped. A container that starts between the inventory and `sparkrun stop` is outside this check.

The load guard treats a ready server differently. A recovered management-link drop is recorded and the timings within 15 s of it are excluded from the analysis; it does not end the load. A management link continuously unhealthy for 30 s, or host or detail telemetry missing for 30 s, ends the load and leaves the server running. Only a confirmed fatal condition stops the candidate, through the same identity check and exact-ID stop against the ranks recorded before the load: a host reboot, a rank that stopped, was OOM-killed or replaced, a kernel OOM, Xid or RoCE failure, HTTP 503 with `ok: false`, or three consecutive samples below 2 GiB available. Neither guard restarts or rolls back.

#### Profile contents and comparisons

`glm53-exl3-tp2-mia-tf-v1.2-bmm-850k` preserves the original v1.2 recipe’s serving, distribution and cache settings, recovered from that retained recipe. Its original configuration passed the October 1 live gates; the newly named file and packaged mod have only local validation. `glm53-exl3-tp2-mia-tf-v1.5-bmm-850k` is the incremental server update: stock TR3 `25a44fdb`, DFlash2 `bf582e4e`, TP2/PP1, C4, an 850k request ceiling, 32,768 default reply budget, 18.5 GiB reserve, 12.5 GiB extra-cache cap and the same vision/input bounds. `glm53-exl3-tp2-mia-tf-v1.5-mia-1m` selects Mia’s new checkpoint and published TP2/C4 serving defaults, including the 1,048,576 window and 14.5 GiB reserve. `glm53-exl3-tp2-mia-tf-v1.8-bmm-850k` is the v1.5 TR3 recipe on the v1.8 image: the same weights, drafter, capacity limits and launcher values, plus one setting, described with the v1.8 provenance below. `glm53-exl3-tp2-mia-tf-v1.8-mia-1m` is the v1.8 kit’s published two-Spark recipe adapted to SparkRun: the same standard Mia checkpoint, drafter and limits as the v1.5 Mia-weights recipe, on the v1.8 image, with every engine setting the v1.8 launcher exports stated in the recipe. `glm53-exl3-tp2-mia-tf-v1.10-mia-1m` is the same adaptation of the v1.10 kit’s recipe: the checkpoint, drafter and limits of the v1.8 Mia-weights recipe on the v1.10 image, with every engine setting the v1.10 launcher exports stated in the recipe. SparkRun’s lifecycle, fabric detection, exact-call-ID/strict-health site guards and 1.2M pool readiness floor remain in the v1.5, v1.8 and v1.10 profiles; these adapt the upstream serving recipe rather than invoking its launcher, clock controls or window fallback, and the differences that leaves are listed with the v1.8 provenance and, for v1.10, in [its section](#v110-mia-weights-candidate). C8, steering/abliteration, the Ablit weights and upstream work not yet merged are outside these profiles.

Two kinds of comparison are available. Within a weight family, across kit releases, the weights and limits stay fixed and the server changes: `glm53-exl3-tp2-mia-tf-v1.2-bmm-850k` against `glm53-exl3-tp2-mia-tf-v1.5-bmm-850k` changes the server and launcher defaults; `glm53-exl3-tp2-mia-tf-v1.5-bmm-850k` against `glm53-exl3-tp2-mia-tf-v1.8-bmm-850k` changes the server patch set and the RoCE wait, and against the measured v1.2 everything the v1.5 comparison does as well; `glm53-exl3-tp2-mia-tf-v1.5-mia-1m` against `glm53-exl3-tp2-mia-tf-v1.8-mia-1m` changes the patch set, the RoCE wait and one NCCL variable described below; `glm53-exl3-tp2-mia-tf-v1.8-mia-1m` against `glm53-exl3-tp2-mia-tf-v1.10-mia-1m` changes the patch set and the eight settings in the v1.10 table, of which only the nucleus setting changes behaviour. Within a kit release, a BMM profile against its Mia profile (`…v1.5-bmm-850k` with `…v1.5-mia-1m`, or `…v1.8-bmm-850k` with `…v1.8-mia-1m`) changes weights, request ceiling and reserve together, so it evaluates complete recipes, not weights. A weights-only conclusion requires matched server settings and capacity limits, which no pair here provides. Keep prompts, sampling, reasoning effort, tool/client behavior and output budgets equal; distinguish cold boots from warm replay and record the profile, git commit, model revision, startup pool and both hosts’ memory minima with each receipt.

The versioned mods are self-contained: each includes its own pinned readiness gate, launcher and guard installer. `prepare.py` verifies five tracked helper hashes without copying from a sibling mod, and `run.sh` repeats that check inside the container. The v1.2 lock accepts the old image’s guarded source, the shared v1.5 mod accepts the v1.5 image’s, the v1.8 mod, shared by both v1.8 profiles, accepts only the v1.8 image’s, and the v1.10 mod accepts only the v1.10 image’s. The v1.10 `prepare.py` also refuses a linked or missing manifest, and its guard installer a missing source file. Image- and model/revision-keyed SparkRun runtime caches give the six profiles separate persistent cache directories; the v1.2 profile’s is the one the original recipe already uses. To add another release, add an explicitly named recipe and a matching pinned bundle when its image or helpers change; retain the existing profiles.

- Launcher: [`start.sh`](https://github.com/MiaAI-Lab/GLM-5.3-Flash-EXL3-2x-DGX-Sparks-TensorFold/blob/33b50fde06fd7ea604cbc6a663880068ab1e2ee4/start.sh) never runs, so its preflight, memory warning, saved logs and smoke test are absent, as are its two recoveries: the retry with a smaller window when the requested one does not fit, and the `SPLIT=0` retry after a first NCCL connection failure ([upstream #36](https://github.com/MiaAI-Lab/GLM-5.3-Flash-EXL3-2x-DGX-Sparks-TensorFold/issues/36)). - Rank command: the mod passes the kit’s serve flags plus `--backend cuda` (upstream leaves the default `auto`) and `--no-update-check` (the kit sets the equivalent variable, as does the recipe).

Server changes the v1.8 image brings over v1.5, from the pinned changelog:

- Kept prompts under shared prefixes and pressure (v1.6): a resume from a shared system prefix copies those rows into free rows instead of taking over the writer's extent ([PR #44](https://github.com/MiaAI-Lab/GLM-5.3-Flash-EXL3-2x-DGX-Sparks-TensorFold/pull/44)); it falls back to the old behaviour when no rows are free or with `TF_GLM_MULTI_LONE=1`, and the recipes keep `0`. - Kept-state cap (v1.7.1): shared-prefix states are evicted by recency, repairing a v1.6 regression in which a new agent run re-read its system prompt once the cap was full ([upstream #75](https://github.com/MiaAI-Lab/GLM-5.3-Flash-EXL3-2x-DGX-Sparks-TensorFold/issues/75)). v1.5 predates that regression.
- Capacity and cancellation (v1.6, v1.8): requests queued behind four busy streams are dropped when their client leaves, and a stream whose delivery fails frees its slot at once. Capacity refusals before response headers are sent answer HTTP 429 with `Retry-After: 5` instead of a bare 503 or 400 ([PR #48](https://github.com/MiaAI-Lab/GLM-5.3-Flash-EXL3-2x-DGX-Sparks-TensorFold/pull/48)), which keeps them distinct from the health guard's 503. Once an SSE response has started, an error stays within that HTTP 200 stream.
- Vision (v1.8): a chat's pictures are decoded once per server instead of every turn, in a host-memory cache that is on by default and capped at 384 MiB of source bytes plus eight canvases ([PR #63](https://github.com/MiaAI-Lab/GLM-5.3-Flash-EXL3-2x-DGX-Sparks-TensorFold/pull/63)); text that only quotes the template's picture marker stays text ([PR #64](https://github.com/MiaAI-Lab/GLM-5.3-Flash-EXL3-2x-DGX-Sparks-TensorFold/pull/64)).
- Replies (v1.6): a raw `<|assistant|>` token now ends the reply instead of reaching the client.
- Memory on a fresh conversation after a long one (v1.8): the kept-state take-over clones only the states it keeps ([PR #71](https://github.com/MiaAI-Lab/GLM-5.3-Flash-EXL3-2x-DGX-Sparks-TensorFold/pull/71)). - Capacity tuning. These use the published 16 streams and 10 GiB reserve, not the C4 shape and the 8 GiB headroom goal the GLM evaluation was built around.

### Token usage accounting

The refreshed Mia recipe includes [`--enable-prompt-tokens-details`](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/cli/serve/#--enable-prompt-tokens-details). It exposes `usage.prompt_tokens_details.cached_tokens` in API responses and is independent of `--enable-prefix-caching`. TensorFold exposes the same usage breakdown from session reuse. `usage.prompt_tokens` includes both cached and fresh input; a missing cache breakdown does not mean every prompt token was freshly processed.

The [September 26 usage investigation](https://app.t3.codes/085bbae7-4da9-4bfa-86ed-ac1a17ad3763/8c1a694a-b03e-4b5d-80f1-79b1d9f778e4) confirmed that the previous GLM launch omitted this flag: vLLM recorded cache reuse internally, while OpenCode stored the whole prompt as ordinary input and Codeburn lost that distinction. Each used one tool read plus a continuation and produced three API calls. TensorFold recorded 8,276 ordinary input, 155 output and 11,648 cache-read tokens (0/3,456/8,192 per call). Refreshed vLLM recorded 10,056 ordinary input, 136 output and 9,856 cache-read tokens (0/2,688/7,168 per call). Codeburn matched every saved OpenCode call’s token breakdown and timestamp on each engine. Generated replies and checkpoint boundaries differ, so these totals are accounting evidence rather than a matched token-savings benchmark. This establishes that client/provider path, not arbitrary clients or renamed tool IDs. The saved client ceiling is 850,000 context / 32,000 output; the isolated checks use 32,768 output. TensorFold receipts are `client-summary.json`, `saved-messages.json` and `codeburn-export.json`; vLLM receipts use the `mia-vllm-v7-client-20261001f-` prefix.

After enabling it, repeat an identical long prompt while its prefix is still cached and confirm a warm response reports nonzero `usage.prompt_tokens_details.cached_tokens`. ## Settings and limits

The [recipe](mia-glm53-exl3.yaml) keeps InstantTensor, EXL3/TR3 target TP2, DFlash2 k=7 with draft TP2, 850,000 context, four sequences, 7,168-token batches, FP8 KV, CUDA graphs, fair mixed-prefill scheduling, and abliteration disabled. Image and model revisions are immutable pins. Source and image are pinned separately; the source revision is not a claim about image build provenance.

`INSTANTTENSOR_BUFFER_SIZE=2147483648` caps loader I/O staging at 2 GiB instead of the installed InstantTensor 0.2.0 TP2 default of 4 GiB. The library automatically reduces I/O depth; the largest target tensor is about 1.18 GiB. This is a loading bound, not a promise of 2 GiB lower steady-state RAM. The refreshed Mia recipe's explicit 11 GiB KV pool governs serving allocation instead of the `.85` utilization setting; the retained control uses 14 GiB. Eight NCCL channels, right-sized indexer workspace, a 1 GiB multimodal processor cache and graph estimation disabled remain; graph execution stays enabled. Dense FP8 `all`, BF16 large-M KDA and compact draft KV follow the coherent current upstream bundle.

W20 uses q4mse non-experts, latent FP8 KV, multi-slot prefill, W19 scratch/NCCL settings, W20 tools, a 2 GiB RAM session store and a 64 GiB NVMe tier per rank. Its shared pool reserves prompt plus output plus 64 tokens per request. Mia TensorFold uses its separate q4/FP8/chunked-KDA, DFlash2/copy-draft and multi-prefill bundle; dense weights and KV are lossy and chunked KDA changes arithmetic. Its pool admits aligned prompt extents and grows them during decode. The 850k ceiling describes one request while pool space is available; slot admission, memory minima, cold-prefill latency and actual decode overlap must be measured. Vision is enabled in all candidates; logprobs and maximum-image/context capabilities are not assumed equivalent.

SparkRun's fit estimate does not account for the full hybrid cache state; use vLLM allocation and host telemetry to judge fit. The 850k allocation/retrieval test completed with cache preemptions and replay, not uninterrupted prefill. 850k is a per-request ceiling, not a promise of four simultaneous 850k requests or responsive long-prefill latency. Idle prefix retention is much smaller than equivalent-token capacity. The vLLM 48-image/1-video and Mia TensorFold v1.2 50-image/4-video maxima, simultaneous maximum-context workloads, general long-context quality and reboot recovery have not been qualified. Mia v1.2 bounds request-wide image/video tokens to 16,384/32,768; our media gate checks five images, not those full maxima.
